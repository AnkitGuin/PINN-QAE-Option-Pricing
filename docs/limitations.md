# Limitations

Honest accounting of what this implementation does and does not support.

## Scope exclusions (by design, per the research specification)

- American/exotic options; Heston or other stochastic-volatility models;
  reinforcement learning; multi-asset options; real quantum hardware;
  quantum neural networks; quantum PINNs; joint unrestricted volatility +
  rate learning; unrestricted future-volatility forecasting.
- No claims are made about quantum advantage, speedup, or hardware
  feasibility. Simulator resource counts and timings are accounting facts,
  not performance claims.

## Data limitations

- **All quantitative results are synthetic.** Experiment 3 (real market data)
  is not executed: no suitable historical European-option dataset is
  accessible in the execution environment (no API credentials, no licensed
  vendor files). Per the specification, unsuitable data was not substituted.
- Synthetic option surfaces are generated under Black–Scholes dynamics with
  piecewise-constant volatility schedules — the same model family the
  calibrators assume. This is by design (the research question is about
  estimation, not model misspecification), but it means the comparison says
  nothing about behaviour under real market microstructure, smiles, jumps or
  volatility risk premia.

## Methodological limitations

- **PINN formulation.** The original 4-input formulation V(S,τ,K,r) hit a
  representation floor (~0.35 RMSE at the profile optimum); the implemented
  homogeneous formulation V = S·u(x,τ,r) removes the floor. This is a
  documented deviation in parameterisation, not in the research question,
  and the final report records the dead end in full.
- **Single shared σ per window.** The inverse PINN estimates one effective
  volatility per calibration window (as does LS). Time-varying σ within a
  window is represented only through the integrated-variance pricing form.
- **Static hyperparameters.** The PINN protocol was frozen after development
  tuning on development scenarios (documented as such), before running the
  evaluation conditions. No per-condition tuning was performed.
- **Statistical power.** 5 seeds per condition, 20 chronological blocks per
  seed. Paired-difference intervals use a normal approximation and ignore
  autocorrelation; they are reported as descriptive, not confirmatory.
- **LS is exactly optimal on clean constant synthetic data** (prices are
  generated by the same BS formula LS inverts). The research question is
  therefore about robustness to noise, sparsity and change — not about
  beating an exactly-invertible generator on clean data.

## Quantum limitations

- The A operator uses exact `StatePreparation`, which embeds both
  distribution loading and payoff encoding; its transpiled depth/size is
  large (reported in `resources.json`). No claim is made that this
  state-preparation approach is efficient or hardware-practical.
- QAE runs on an ideal (noiseless) statevector simulator; no hardware noise
  model, no error mitigation, no real-device execution.
- The QAE variant is canonical Brassard et al. QAE. MLAE/AE without phase
  estimation were not implemented.
- Discretization bounds are quantile-based (total excluded mass α = 0.001 by
  default). The in-range law is renormalized for QAE; truncation and grid
  approximation both contribute to the separately reported discretization
  error.

## Engineering limitations

- CPU-only, 2-core execution environment; training budgets were chosen for
  student-scale compute (modest collocation counts, limited search, 5 seeds).
- PyTorch and Qiskit-Aer cannot both load in a single process on this
  platform (static-TLS/libgomp conflict). The experiment scripts never import
  both; the test suite runs circuit-level quantum checks in a dedicated
  subprocess (`tests/_quantum_runner.py`). This is an environment constraint,
  not a scientific one.
- The enabled real-data path of exp3 is implemented but has never been
  exercised end-to-end on an eligible dataset; treat it as unvalidated until
  run against one.
